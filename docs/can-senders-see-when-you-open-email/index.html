Can senders see when you open their email?

Updated November 6, 2026. Written by the developer of Unseen, a Gmail tracker blocker.

Usually, yes. Most newsletters, shop emails and sales emails tell the sender whether you opened them, when, how many times, and sometimes on what kind of device. Personal emails from friends usually don't, unless they use a tracking add-on.

How it works

The email contains a tiny image, often 1×1 pixel and invisible, with an address that's unique to you. When you open the email, your email app downloads that image, and the sender's server writes down: this person opened it, at this time. Open it again tomorrow, and that's logged too.

Links can be tracked the same way. A tracked link first goes to the sender's server, which records the click, and then forwards you to the real page.

What the sender actually sees

  • That you opened it, and when. Every time, not just the first.
  • Which links you clicked.
  • Your rough location and device, in some email apps. In Gmail, Google's image proxy hides your IP address and location, but not the open itself.

Sales tools use this to time follow-ups ("you opened my proposal three times, ready to talk?"), and some employers use it as a hidden read receipt.

How to check a single email

In Gmail, open the email, click the three dots, choose Show original, and look for image addresses with long random codes, often from a mailing company's domain. It works, but it's slow. A blocker extension can name the tracker for you.

How to stop it in Gmail

  • Built in, free: in Gmail's settings, set Images to Ask before displaying external images. Trackers stop, but so does every normal picture until you click. Step-by-step guide.
  • An extension that blocks only trackers: images keep loading and only the pixels are blocked. We compare the main ones, including ones that aren't ours.

Questions

Is this legal?

It depends where you and the sender are. In the EU, senders generally need your consent, and Italy's and France's data protection authorities spelled this out for email pixels in 2026. In the US it's mostly allowed.

Will the sender know I blocked it?

No. They see the same as for someone reading with images turned off: no open.

What about Apple Mail and Outlook?

Apple Mail's "Protect Mail Activity" loads images in advance through Apple's servers, which makes open tracking unreliable. Outlook can be set not to download pictures automatically.
